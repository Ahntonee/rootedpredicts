One of the easier markets to wager on in football betting is Both Teams to Score, commonly known as BTTS.

The question is simple: will both teams score at least one goal each during the match?

If yes, the BTTS Yes bet wins. If no, the BTTS No bet wins.

For example:

Arsenal 2-1 Chelsea - Arsenal scored, Chelsea scored. Both teams to score is a Yes.

Liverpool 3-0 Everton - Liverpool scored, Everton did not. Both teams to score is a No.

For this reason, individuals will look up what does both teams to score mean, both teams to score yes and no, and both teams to score gg ng.

BTTS Yes / GG means both teams scored.

BTTS No / NG means a team failed to score.

Football today is more attacking than ever, and a lot of matches are open at both ends. That's part of why BTTS is such a popular market.

It also doesn't matter who wins the game. Your team could be losing 2-1, but as long as both sides find the net, your BTTS Yes bet is still standing. That's a market you can enjoy even if the outcome of the market doesn't go your way. BTTS also works well alongside other markets. One common mistake in BTTS betting is looking at just one stat. For example, a team might have conceded in 4 matches in a row, and a bettor sees that and assumes BTTS is automatic next time. Football doesn't work like that. At Rootedpredict, we look at several angles at once:

1. Current goal-scoring form. Are both sides scoring regularly? A team scoring in 8 of its last 10 matches paints a very different picture from one that has only scored in 3 of its last 10.

2. Defensive flaws.BTTS is not just about attack, a broken defense is as important. Let’s say one side scores in 9 of 10 matches but also concedes in 8 of those. If the opposition scores in 7 of their last 10 and also concedes regularly, that's a strong BTTS combination.

3. Home and away splits. Home and away form can matter a lot just like scoring form.  Some clubs look unstoppable at home and completely different on the road, and vice versa. We always check how the home side performs at home and how the away side performs away, since this small detail can be the difference between a BTTS match and a one-sided result.

4. Head-to-head history. Some fixtures are fun of finishing 3-2 or 2-1 one after another. If the two sides have a history of high-scoring open meetings, then that should be taken into account.

5. Team news. A weakened defense because of injuries or suspensions can push the odds of BTTS up, while a missing key striker can pull them back down. We always check the squad news before finalising a pick.

BTTS and Over 2.5 goals often get grouped because similar factors tend to support both markets.they tend to perform in a similar manner. But it is not the same and it's not a good idea to confuse them

BTTS:

- Both teams need to score at least one goal each.

- 1-1 is a winning scoreline.

- 2-1 is a winning scoreline.

- 3-1 is a winning scoreline.

Over 2.5:

- The match must have a minimum of 3 goals.

- 2-1 is a winning scoreline.

- 3-0 is a winning scoreline.

- 1-1 is not a winning scoreline for Over 2.5.

That last point here is the key difference. A 1-1 result is a BTTS win but an Over 2.5 loss. A 2-1 result is a winner for both markets. This is exactly why some bettors combine BTTS with Over 2.5, but it also shows why each should be judged as its own distinct outcome rather than lumped together automatically.

Another popular combination bet is Both Teams to Score and Win. This is different from a plain BTTS bet, since it needs two things to happen together: your chosen team must win, and both teams must score.

For instance, backing Team A to win and BTTS:

Team A 2-1 Team B — WIN (Team A won, both teams scored)

Team A 1-0 Team B — LOSS (Team A won, but Team B didn't score)

Team A 1-1 Team B — LOSS (both teams scored, but Team A didn't win)
